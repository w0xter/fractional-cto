# Fractional CTO — ICP & Acquisition Playbook v1

## 1. Objective

Build a repeatable acquisition system capable of generating a small number of highly qualified Fractional CTO opportunities.

The objective is **not maximum lead volume**.

The service only has capacity for approximately 2–3 simultaneous clients, so the commercial system should optimize for:

- relevance;
- urgency;
- ability to pay;
- technical leverage;
- founder quality;
- recurring engagement potential.

The operating principle is:

> **Capture broadly enough to see the market, qualify aggressively and only spend time on situations where senior technical ownership creates disproportionate value.**

This adapts the principle previously used by Nomu:

> Capture broadly, qualify efficiently and prioritize outreach selectively.

---

# 2. Positioning

## Core positioning

### Fractional CTO for startups that have already started building

**From prototype, pilots or early traction to a product and engineering organisation that can scale.**

I work directly with founders who have moved beyond the idea stage but are not ready — or do not yet need — to hire a full-time CTO.

Typical scope:

**Product Discovery · Architecture · AI Strategy · Technical Prototyping · Engineering Delivery · Hiring**

The service sits between two alternatives:

**Hiring developers without senior technical ownership**

and

**Hiring a full-time CTO before the company actually needs one.**

---

# 3. Core commercial thesis

The main problem is not lack of development capacity.

It is lack of **technical ownership**.

Developers can execute tasks.

Agencies can build software.

Freelancers can add capacity.

But somebody still needs to decide:

- What should we actually build?
- What should we not build yet?
- Is the architecture appropriate for the stage of the company?
- Which technical risks matter?
- What should remain deterministic and where should AI be used?
- Is the roadmap technically realistic?
- What should be built internally?
- What should be bought or outsourced?
- Who should we hire next?
- Is the existing team performing effectively?
- Which technical debt matters now?
- Are we creating unnecessary complexity?
- What needs to be prototyped before we commit months of engineering effort?

The Fractional CTO owns those decisions with the founder.

---

# 4. ICP architecture

We will initially operate with **two ICP routes only**.

Avoid creating many vertical-specific ICPs until there is enough commercial data to justify them.

The two routes are:

1. **Build**
2. **Scale**

---

# 5. ICP 1 — Build

## Short definition

Founder-led startups that have already demonstrated execution or commercial intent but still need to turn a prototype, pilot or validated problem into a serious technical product.

This ICP inherits most of the previous Nomu **Launch ICP**.

---

## Company profile

Typical characteristics:

- Pre-seed or seed.
- 1–15 employees.
- 0–4 developers.
- Software is central to the business.
- Fast decision-making.
- Founder still directly involved in product.
- B2B SaaS, AI or software-enabled company preferred.

Geography initially:

- Spain.
- Remote European opportunities where communication and working model fit.

---

## Required evidence of execution

At least one strong signal should already exist:

- Working prototype.
- MVP.
- No-code product.
- Pilot agreed with a customer.
- Paying customer.
- Active beta users.
- Commercial conversations with clear demand.
- Funding raised.
- Accelerator participation plus real execution.
- Manual version of the service already being delivered.
- A business process that users are already paying to solve.

The key rule inherited from Nomu:

> **We do not optimize for founders who merely have an idea.**

---

## Typical situation

Examples:

- “We have three pilots signed but the product is still a prototype.”
- “We built the first version with Lovable / Bubble / Replit.”
- “The demo works, but I don't know how to turn it into a production system.”
- “We have customers waiting and no technical co-founder.”
- “An agency built the MVP and now we need to own the technology.”
- “We raised pre-seed and need to decide what engineering team to build.”
- “We need to ship something credible before the next fundraising milestone.”
- “We want to introduce AI, but don't know which parts actually should use AI.”

---

## Primary buyer

Usually:

- Founder.
- CEO.
- Business co-founder.
- Product founder without senior technical leadership.

Possible influencers:

- Investor.
- Advisor.
- Accelerator.
- Existing freelance developer.
- Product lead.
- First engineer.

---

## Primary pains

### Pain 1 — Nobody owns the technology

The company has people who can implement things, but nobody is accountable for the technical system as a whole.

Symptoms:

- Decisions made locally without architecture.
- Founder evaluating technical decisions without enough context.
- Providers controlling important technical knowledge.
- Nobody knows whether the current approach will survive the next stage.

---

### Pain 2 — They don't know what to build first

Symptoms:

- Large roadmap.
- Too many features.
- Unclear MVP.
- Prototype becoming production by accident.
- Constant scope changes.
- Technical effort invested before important assumptions are validated.

---

### Pain 3 — Prototype ≠ product

Symptoms:

- Fragile workflows.
- Poor permissions/security.
- Manual operations behind the UI.
- No clear deployment process.
- Weak observability.
- AI workflows difficult to evaluate.
- Technical shortcuts becoming operational problems.

---

### Pain 4 — They need technical credibility

Triggers may include:

- customer pilots;
- enterprise conversations;
- fundraising;
- due diligence;
- first senior hires.

The founder needs someone who can credibly represent the technology.

---

### Pain 5 — They need speed without creating a mess

They do not need enterprise architecture.

They need architecture appropriate to the next 12–24 months.

---

# 6. ICP 2 — Scale

## Short definition

Startups that already have a product and some engineering capacity, but have outgrown founder-led technical coordination and still do not justify a permanent senior CTO.

This route incorporates the most relevant parts of the previous **Operations ICP**, but deliberately excludes generic process digitalisation.

---

## Company profile

Typical characteristics:

- Seed / early Series A.
- 5–30 employees.
- 2–8 engineers.
- Product already in production.
- Customers or recurring usage.
- Growing roadmap.
- Engineering complexity increasing.

---

## Typical situation

Examples:

- “We have four developers but every technical decision still reaches the founder.”
- “Delivery is becoming unpredictable.”
- “We've hired engineers but don't have engineering leadership.”
- “Our agency/freelancers know more about the product than we do internally.”
- “We're seeing incidents and technical debt.”
- “We're preparing to hire our first Engineering Lead.”
- “We need someone to assess whether the current architecture can support growth.”
- “We have product-market signals, but Engineering isn't keeping up.”

---

## Primary buyer

- CEO.
- Founder.
- COO in software-enabled businesses.
- Head of Product.
- Occasionally an investor introducing the company.

---

## Primary pains

### Pain 1 — Founder is the technical bottleneck

The founder is simultaneously doing:

- product;
- clients;
- fundraising;
- architecture;
- developers;
- delivery.

This is unsustainable.

---

### Pain 2 — Developers lack senior direction

They may be individually competent but need:

- architecture;
- prioritisation;
- technical standards;
- escalation support;
- mentorship;
- better scope.

---

### Pain 3 — Delivery has become difficult to predict

Signals:

- repeated missed commitments;
- QA late in the cycle;
- unclear ownership;
- scope constantly moving;
- rework;
- incidents interrupting delivery.

---

### Pain 4 — Hiring decisions are becoming expensive

The company does not know:

- whether it needs frontend/backend/full-stack;
- senior vs junior;
- employee vs contractor;
- Tech Lead vs CTO;
- whether current engineers are strong enough.

---

### Pain 5 — Technical debt is becoming business debt

The founder can feel that something is wrong but cannot prioritise it.

---

# 7. Explicit non-ICP

Disqualification is a feature of the business model.

## Reject or nurture if:

- Only an idea exists.
- No validation has occurred.
- There is no realistic budget.
- They want an equity-only technical co-founder.
- They want someone to code a predefined backlog.
- They need 4–6 full-time developers more than technical leadership.
- They want “AI strategy” without a concrete problem.
- They expect free product discovery.
- They cannot identify a decision-maker.
- There is no meaningful urgency.
- The project is primarily a website or standard implementation.
- Technology is not strategically important.
- They refuse to reduce scope.
- They expect 24/7 CTO availability.
- The problem requires months of business consultancy before technical work can begin.

Important inherited Nomu lesson:

> **Do not optimise acquisition around aspirational founders.**

---

# 8. Qualification scoring

Score each opportunity from **0–10**.

Five dimensions, 0–2 points each.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Validation | Idea only | Prototype / conversations | Pilots / customers / revenue |
| Technical pain | Vague | Recognisable | Concrete and urgent |
| Budget | No credible budget | Unclear | Can sustain €4k–€7k+/month |
| Authority | Intermediary | Founder involved | Decision-maker directly engaged |
| Timing | Someday | 3–6 months | <90 days / active trigger |

### Interpretation

**8–10 — Priority**

Respond quickly. Founder call.

**6–7 — Potential**

Explore if signal is strong. Often good Technical Clarity Sprint candidates.

**0–5 — Nurture / Reject**

Do not spend significant founder time.

---

## Additional binary question

Every opportunity must answer:

> **Can Luis create disproportionate value here?**

Yes / No.

Examples of **No**:

- Just needs frontend capacity.
- Generic WordPress work.
- Mature company needs a full-time VP Engineering.
- Highly specialised infrastructure beyond our relevant expertise.
- There is already an effective CTO.

# 9. Trigger events

Triggers matter more than static firmographics.

## Build triggers

- Pilot signed.
- First paying customer.
- Funding announced.
- Accelerator acceptance.
- Product launched.
- Prototype publicly shared.
- Founder asking for technical co-founder.
- Founder hiring first developer.
- Moving away from no-code.
- Enterprise customer asking for security/reliability changes.
- Deadline connected to a commercial opportunity.

## Scale triggers

- Hiring multiple engineers.
- Hiring Engineering Lead / CTO.
- Recent funding.
- Production incident.
- Public complaints about product performance.
- Rapid headcount growth.
- Migration/rebuild announcement.
- Agency relationship ending.
- Founder publicly discussing engineering bottlenecks.
- New product line.
- Enterprise customers increasing technical requirements.

# 10. Offer ladder

The funnel should be:

**Signal → Fit Call → Technical Clarity Sprint → 3-Month Fractional CTO → Extend / Hire / Exit**

---

## Fit Call

30–40 minutes.

Objective:

**Qualify, not solve.**

Determine:

- Build or Scale?
- Is the pain real?
- Why now?
- What already exists?
- Who owns technical decisions?
- What is commercially at stake?
- Budget?
- Timing?
- Is there a real Fractional CTO problem?

Do not produce:

- architecture;
- detailed roadmap;
- technical audit;
- detailed estimates.

---

## Technical Clarity Sprint

1–2 weeks.

Indicative price:

**€2.5k–€3.5k fixed**

Potential outputs:

- Product/Technical Brief.
- Architecture.
- Technical Risk Map.
- 90-Day Roadmap.
- Build / Buy / Delay decisions.
- Team requirements.
- Technical prototype where uncertainty justifies it.

This adapts the previous Nomu Pre-Kickstart logic.

Important principle retained from previous commercial feedback:

> Fixed-scope clarity can reduce buyer uncertainty.

---

## Fractional CTO

Initial engagement:

**3 months**

Approximate commitment:

**~1 day/week**

Indicative initial pricing hypothesis:

**€3.5k–€4.5k/month**

Core responsibilities:

- Founder sync.
- Technical roadmap.
- Architecture.
- Engineering planning.
- Technical/design reviews.
- Delivery risk.
- Hiring.
- Vendor/agency management.
- Production escalation.
- AI strategy when relevant.

---

## Fractional CTO + Build

Approximate commitment:

**1.5–2 days/week**

Indicative hypothesis:

**€5.5k–€7.5k/month**

Principle:

> **I implement uncertainty, not backlog.**

Hands-on work is reserved for:

- prototypes;
- uncertain architecture;
- difficult integrations;
- LLM / AI workflows;
- technical bottlenecks;
- production debugging;
- migrations;
- high-leverage infrastructure.

# 11. Acquisition strategy

Initial acquisition should prioritise **speed and signal**, not brand building.

## Priority 1 — Existing network & old Nomu pipeline

This should be the fastest channel.

Review:

- Lost opportunities.
- Postponed opportunities.
- Founders who chose to hire internally.
- Former clients.
- Advisors.
- Partners.
- Investors.
- People who previously asked Nomu for technical guidance.

Particularly interesting:

Leads that previously chose:

> **“Hire CTO/internal team instead of outsourcing.”**

Those were lost opportunities for an agency.

They may be perfect opportunities for Fractional CTO.

> “We have traction, but we don't really have a CTO.”

One good ecosystem relationship can create multiple opportunities.

# 15. Outreach principles

The previous Nomu outbound had the right principle:

> Relevant → Human → Helpful → Conversation → Commercial opportunity

Keep it.

The first message should not contain the full Fractional CTO pitch.

It should answer:

**Why am I contacting this person now?**

---

# 16. Founder outreach — Build

Example:

> Hey Marta — I came across the product you've been building and had a look at the first version. It looks like you've already moved quite a bit beyond the idea stage.
>
> Out of curiosity, are you currently more focused on validating the product, or on turning what you have into something ready for broader customer use?

If relevant:

> I saw that the first version was built with Lovable and that you're already talking to customers. I've worked through that prototype → production transition several times and it tends to create some interesting technical decisions.
>
> Do you already have someone owning architecture/engineering internally?

---

# 17. Founder outreach — Scale

Example:

> Hey Javier — I saw you're growing the engineering side of [Company].
>
> I've spent the last few years going through a similar transition: from founder-led technical decisions to squads and engineering leads.
>
> Curious: are you still personally owning most technical decisions, or has someone started taking that responsibility internally?

Again:

**conversation first, offer second.**

---

# 18. VC / accelerator outreach

Example:

> Hi [Name] — after five years building products and engineering teams at Nomu Labs, I'm opening capacity to work with a small number of early-stage companies as a Fractional CTO.
>
> The situations where I tend to be most useful are companies that already have a prototype, pilots or first customers, but aren't yet ready to hire a full-time CTO.
>
> I can step in around architecture, technical roadmap, AI/product decisions, delivery and first engineering hires for a few months.
>
> If you have any portfolio company in that transition, happy to be useful.

# 21. Pipeline stages

Use a deliberately simple pipeline:

1. **Signal identified**
2. **Qualified for outreach**
3. **Conversation started**
4. **Fit Call booked**
5. **Fit Call completed**
6. **Clarity Sprint proposed**
7. **Clarity Sprint won**
8. **Fractional CTO proposed**
9. **Fractional CTO won**
10. **Active**
11. **Nurture**
12. **Lost / Disqualified**

Every active opportunity must have:

> **Next action + next action date**

No exceptions.

- Clarity Sprints won.
- Fractional engagements proposed.

---

## Conversion metrics

Track:

**Positive replies / targeted outreach**

**Fit Calls / positive conversations**

**Qualified opportunities / Fit Calls**

**Paid Sprint / qualified opportunities**

**Fractional CTO / paid Sprints**

> **Qualified Fractional CTO opportunities per 100 targeted prospects.**

Not leads/hour.

# 23. Capacity metric

This business needs a metric Nomu did not need in exactly the same way:

> **Founder capacity committed**

Track monthly:

- Client.
- € / month.
- Days/week.
- Days/month.
- Expected async overhead.
- Contract end.
- Renewal probability.

Target:

**12–14 client days/month maximum**

until there is evidence that a higher load remains sustainable.

Do not maximise utilisation.

Compare:

- Warm network.

- Was pricing resisted?
- Did prospects want advisory or actual execution?

Then update:

**ICP v2 + messaging v2 + pricing v2.**

# 26. First 30-day commercial goal

Do not set the initial goal as:

> “Reach €10k MRR.”

The first milestone should be:

### Validation milestone

**Close one paid Technical Clarity Sprint or one 3-month Fractional CTO engagement with a client that matches the intended ICP.**

A second strong outcome would be:

**Create 3+ qualified opportunities from at least two different acquisition channels.**

That would prove more than generating a large lead database.

# 27. Hypotheses to validate

These are not yet established facts.

## H1

Startups with pilots/customers but no CTO will understand and pay for the service more easily than idea-stage founders.

## H2

Build will create more initial demand than Scale.

## H3

Accelerators and VCs will outperform generic founder outbound because trust is transferred.

## H4

A €2.5k–€3.5k Technical Clarity Sprint will reduce friction before a €4k–€7k/month retainer.

## H5

Luis's ability to personally prototype difficult technical components will differentiate the offer from advisory-only Fractional CTOs.

## H6

AI/no-code founders transitioning from prototype to production will be particularly strong prospects.

## H7

Existing Nomu relationships will produce the fastest first client.

These must be tested rather than assumed.
